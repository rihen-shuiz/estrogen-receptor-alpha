# SV08b - Literature for the class questions (VDR superagonists, SERD H12, pocket volumes)

Step SV08b of docs/sv/RUN_SV1028.md (addition 2026-11-04). Written 2026-11-08.

**How this was made.** WebSearch to find candidate sources, WebFetch to open each one. Every claim below comes
from text that was opened in this session; the "source" column says what was opened (publisher/PMC full text,
preprint full text, abstract record, or a PDB entry page). WebFetch returns the opened page through a reader
model that caps verbatim quotes at 125 characters, so claims are recorded as short quotes plus close paraphrase
of the opened text, never from the search snippets.

**Rule 1.9.** Unlike SV08, this step may use claims framed by ligand action class (agonist, superagonist,
partial agonist, SERM, SERD, pure antiestrogen), and does. Wording stays as rule 1.9 requires: crystal
structures show H12 order and position (H12 disorder, H12 mobility), never receptor degradation or cellular
activity; where a source links a structural state to degradation, the file says whether that link was measured
or asserted. Our own classes (SV12) are provisional ChEMBL-mechanism labels and no class here is taken from
structure, cluster or chemistry.

**Stopping.** Q1 was stopped after six sources that agree (three structure papers on three different
superagonist families, one further structure paper, one review, one PDB entry page): another source would not
change the answer. Q2 was stopped after five sources covering crystal, HDX-MS, limited proteolysis and MD, which
agree on the structural side and disagree only on the degradation link - that disagreement is the answer, and is
reported. Q3 was stopped early for a different reason: four opened ERalpha sources give at most two numbers and
none gives a method, and three further sources (searched for a method) gave no volumes at all; the remaining
primary sources for those numbers (Brzozowski 1997 Nature, Shiau 1998 Cell) could not be opened.

## Short answer to the three questions

1. **No - in VDR crystal structures the position of H12 does not differ between superagonists and agonists.**
   Three superagonist families (20-epi MC1288/KH1060, 14-epi TX522, gemini-0072/0097) and the superpotent 2MD
   all give "the position of helix H12 ... strictly maintained" / "the conformation of the LBD is identical",
   with Calpha RMSD 0.08-0.38 A against the reference complex [#1, #3, #4, #6], and a 2019 review states that
   every VDR-LBD/ligand structure reported up to then adopted the active conformation [#7]. Superagonism is
   explained instead by the lifetime of the active complex [#2], by stronger receptor-coactivator interaction
   [#4], and by extra ligand contacts with H12 residues (Val444, Phe448, Leu440) described as "increased
   stabilization of helix H12" [#5, #6]. **For the slide:** "no difference in H12 *position* between VDR
   agonists and superagonists" is supported; "no difference in H12 at all" is not, because #5 and #6 do claim
   extra H12 contacts and better coactivator recruitment - a contact-and-dynamics claim inferred from static
   structures, with no H12 B-factors or MD behind it. In VDR, H12 moves with antagonists and partial agonists,
   not between agonist subtypes [#7].
2. **Yes, H12 destabilisation is reported for SERDs relative to SERMs in ERalpha - in solution more than in the
   coordinates - and the link to degradation is asserted, not demonstrated.** Fulvestrant raises deuterium
   uptake near H12 while E2 protects it, and bazedoxifene stabilises H12 less than 4-OHT [#8]; limited
   proteolysis orders the ligands apo 2.2 < fulvestrant 2.3 < OP-1074 3.5 < 4-OHT 6.0 min [#10]; crystals show
   H12 still docked in the AF-2 cleft with higher B-factors and a disordered H11-H12 loop for long-chain SERDs,
   and the H12 helix starting at 536 rather than 537 [#10, #11]. Against that, one panel study finds SERM and
   SERD structures "very similar" [#12]. On degradation: the same papers measure ERalpha protein loss
   (OP-1074 49 %, fulvestrant 28 % of vehicle) but state that how H12 destabilisation leads to degradation
   "remains unresolved" [#10], another reports "poor correlation with endogenous ERalpha degradation" [#11], and
   a third notes receptor loss "may not be required" for antagonism [#12]. The one case of H12 wholly destabilised
   is ERbeta with ICI164,384 [#13], a different receptor and species.
3. **Reported ERalpha volumes are 450 A3 for the pocket and ~250 A3 for E2/DES, both without a method.** The
   450 A3 cavity is quoted by two opened sources [#15, #16] from the 1997 estradiol structure paper, which could
   not be opened; the 250 A3 ligand volume likewise [#16]; no opened ERalpha source names a program, probe radius
   or grid, and three give no volumes at all [#19]. Relative values exist (a bulky substituent enlarges the pocket
   by 40 % [#17]). The only opened sources that report pocket, ligand and occupancy together are VDR papers
   (660 A3 pocket with 375-392 A3 ligands; 729-776 A3 cavity with 461-474 A3 ligands, "61%, 63%, 63%"), also with
   no program named [#18]. So SV14's references (E2 ligand 223.7 A3, wild-type E2 pocket 956.2 A3, apo 793.2 A3,
   median filling 33.8 %) cannot be compared like-for-like with the literature; the comparable statement is that
   our lower E2-pocket mode (505-579 A3) is near the published 450 A3 while the upper mode (956-1027 A3) is not.

## Gaps

- **No method for any ERalpha volume.** Not one opened source names the program, probe radius or grid behind the
  450 A3 pocket or the 250 A3 ligand volume, and the two papers that computed them could not be opened. SV14's
  numbers therefore cannot be validated against the literature, only placed beside it.
- **No published apo ERalpha pocket volume.** Our 793.2 A3 apo baseline (3 entries) has no literature counterpart;
  the only opened apo-state source gives no volume [#19].
- **No ERalpha counterpart of the VDR superagonist question.** No opened source compares H12 between ERalpha
  agonist subtypes (full vs partial agonists, or agonists of different affinity). The VDR evidence [#1-#7] is the
  nearest analogue, in a different receptor, and SV13 C2a/C2b were not testable in our data.
- **No quantified link between the degree of SERD H12 destabilisation and the degree of degradation.** The only
  opened pairing is two compounds in one paper (trypsin half-life 3.5 vs 2.3 min; ERalpha remaining 49 % vs 28 %)
  [#10], and two other sources report the correlation as poor or the degradation as unnecessary [#11, #12].
- **Our four SERD ligands (7I0, I0V, L5B, ZNM) have no opened primary paper.** For ZNM only the 7MSA entry page
  was opened [#20]; nothing was opened for 7I0, I0V or L5B, so no literature statement in this file is specific to
  the SERD chains SV13 draws.
